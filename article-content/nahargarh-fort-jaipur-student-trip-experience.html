Introduction

Our trip to Nahargarh Fort turned out to be one of the most unforgettable experiences of my life.

At the time it felt like just another casual outing with friends from VGU. That is usually how it works — you do not know which evening is going to matter until much later.

Nobody Would Take Us Up the Hill

The trouble started before we had even left.

We tried booking a ride to Nahargarh Fort and most drivers simply refused, because the fort sits high up in the hills. We waited a long time and tried again and again, and nobody seemed willing. The excitement slowly turned into frustration.

Then, after several failed attempts, one driver finally agreed.

A Stop at Jal Mahal

On the way we asked him to stop for a while at Jal Mahal so we could see it properly and take some pictures.

It looked absolutely beautiful in the evening light. The calm lake, the quiet, the reflection of the palace in the water — the whole scene felt a bit unreal. We laughed, took photographs and teased each other, enjoying those carefree moments without any idea how precious they would turn out to be.

The Climb Into the Aravallis

We carried on in the same cab towards the fort.

The road was all sharp turns, cool wind and views across Jaipur. As we climbed higher into the Aravalli hills, the city lights began to sparkle below us. By the time we reached the fort the evening sky had already started changing colour.

We waited for one friend who was coming separately, and once she arrived the group felt complete.

The Sunset

Walking towards the main area of the fort, we got one of the most beautiful views I have ever seen.

A golden-orange sky spreading over the whole city. Cool breeze on our faces. My friends laughing somewhere behind me. For a little while nothing else mattered at all and we were simply enjoying being alive.

The Monkeys

While we were exploring the fort we ran into something we had not planned for.

A large group of monkeys was roaming the area freely, snatching food and belongings straight out of tourists' hands. Some people were being chased. It looked funny at first, and then we watched the chaos a bit more closely and started being careful ourselves.

It was unexpected, and honestly it added to the whole thing.

The Auto Ride Back, and the Tiger

Getting back down was as hard as getting up. There were barely any cabs near the fort at night, so eventually we booked an auto-rickshaw towards Jal Mahal.

That ride became the most thrilling part of the entire journey.

As the auto moved through the dark mountain roads, the driver started telling us about tigers that wander the nearby forest areas late at night. One of my friends casually mentioned seeing something move near the roadside — and the driver immediately went serious. He told everyone to stay quiet and switched off the auto's lights for a few moments. Then he called someone and warned them to be careful travelling through the area.

Silence filled the auto. Dark roads, the hills, and the real possibility of a tiger somewhere nearby. All of us were genuinely shaken.

We never actually saw one. The suspense was enough.

When we finally reached Jal Mahal safely we all started laughing again — though everyone was still quietly thinking about what had just happened.

What I Did Not Know Then

Later that night we wandered around Jal Mahal again, enjoyed the quiet, and eventually booked a cab home. We got back tired and happy, carrying more memories than we realised.

Looking at that trip now, the most beautiful part was not the fort, or the sunset, or the adventure. It was the people.

None of us knew that life would change afterwards. Friend groups shifted, priorities shifted, everyone got busy with their own lives. Some friendships became distant and others faded naturally. The people I laughed with the most that evening are not as close to me today as they were then.

Perhaps that is just how life works.

Conclusion

That trip still stays close to my heart despite all of it.

Sometimes the best memories are the ones we never realise are becoming memories while we are living them. That evening at Nahargarh Fort will always be one of those for me.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How do students get to Nahargarh Fort from Jaipur?

It is harder than it looks. Many cab drivers refuse the trip because the fort is high in the Aravalli hills, and there are barely any cabs near the fort at night — this group came back by auto-rickshaw.

Q2. Is Nahargarh Fort worth visiting at sunset?

Yes. This student describes the sunset over the city, with a golden-orange sky spreading across Jaipur, as one of the most beautiful views he has ever seen.

Q3. Are there monkeys at Nahargarh Fort?

Yes, and they are a genuine nuisance. A large group roams freely, snatching food and belongings from tourists, and some visitors were chased. Keep your bags closed.

Q4. What can you see on the way to Nahargarh Fort?

Jal Mahal is on the route and worth stopping for, particularly in the evening light when the palace reflects in the lake.

Q5. Is it safe to travel down from the fort at night?

Transport is the main difficulty — very few cabs. The auto driver on this trip also spoke about tigers in the nearby forest areas at night, which unsettled the group, though they saw nothing.
