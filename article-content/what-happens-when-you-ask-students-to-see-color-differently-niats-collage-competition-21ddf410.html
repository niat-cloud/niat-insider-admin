The Media Club at NIAT × NSRIT hosted a Color Collage Competition that asked students to think about colour as a way of telling a story. The interest was in how ordinary campus scenes could express a mood.

More Than Just Looking Good

Taking a photo is easy, but making someone feel an emotion through a photo is hard. The leaders of the competition wanted students to move past just making things look nice.

They wanted students to think about how different colors make us feel. For example, does a bright red building make you feel excited or nervous? Does a blue sky feel calm or lonely? The competition forced students to think before they clicked the button.

Finding Beauty in Ordinary Things

What did the students actually capture? They did not go to fancy locations. Instead, they took photos of everyday things around the campus.

They took pictures of campus buildings, shadows, and natural light. But because they were focusing on color, they turned these normal objects into art. A simple corner of a building suddenly looked like a special geometric shape because of how the light hit it.

Making Choices About Framing and Colour

This event showed the big difference between knowing how to use a camera and having the heart of an artist.

The Art of Choosing

Anyone can learn which buttons to press on a camera. But it takes real skill to know what to include in the photo and what to leave out. The students had to make specific choices about the edges of their frames to create the best photographs.

Using Color for Emotion

The students used color to show "temperature". A golden sunset light gave a warm, happy feeling, while cool green and blue shadows gave a quiet, peaceful feeling. They proved that color is not just decoration; it is a way to share a mood.

Why Student Clubs Make Campus Life Better

Taking photos seems easy because everyone has a camera on their phone. But this competition proved that real photography requires deep thinking.

This is why joining student clubs, like the Media Club, is such an important part of your student life. These activities teach you how to think differently, share your ideas without speaking, and look closer at the world around you. An exercise like this can encourage students to look more closely at familiar surroundings.

Seeing Familiar Places Differently

The competition gave students a reason to pause over buildings, shadows and light that might otherwise pass unnoticed. Its lesson was in observation: choosing a frame and a palette can turn a familiar campus scene into a way of expressing an idea.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the competition’s focus?

Students were asked to use colour and composition to tell a story or express a mood, rather than simply capture a pretty picture.

What subjects did participants photograph?

The account describes everyday campus buildings, shadows and natural light.

What did framing contribute?

Deciding what to include and leave out helped students guide attention and shape the feeling of an image.

How did colour help convey emotion in the photographs?

The account describes warm golden light and cooler green or blue shadows as ways to create different feelings. Colour worked alongside the subject and framing.

What made the exercise different from taking an ordinary snapshot?

Students had to pay attention to what they were including, how light fell and what mood the image communicated, rather than only recording a scene.
