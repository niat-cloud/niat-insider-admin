During my first year at NIAT, I began to see adaptability as a practical skill. Planning helped, but projects and conversations did not always follow the plan. Progress often meant learning something new and changing my approach.

What Adaptability Means to Me

Adaptability is the ability to adjust to changing circumstances while continuing to work toward a goal. In technology, that can mean updating knowledge or reconsidering a tool. In a team, it can mean listening to another way of solving a problem.

It also changes how I think about uncertainty. I cannot predict every difficulty, but I can get better at responding when one appears.

Lessons From My AI Project

The AI-powered infrastructure monitoring and failure prediction project I have been developing made this concrete. New challenges emerged, approaches needed revision and learning continued throughout the work.

I had to update decisions as information changed. Staying attached to the first idea would not have helped. Experimenting and adjusting became part of the development process.

Persistence Needed a Changing Strategy

The BRAVE Competition reinforced the same lesson. Nearly 15 unsuccessful business negotiations came before success on the 16th attempt.

Communication and strategy needed to evolve. The useful part of persistence was carrying lessons from one attempt into the next, rather than assuming repetition alone would be enough.

Why Adaptability Matters in Teams

People bring different experiences, perspectives and working styles. Being willing to adjust makes it easier to understand those differences and work toward a shared outcome.

I connect that with leadership too. When conditions change, a team benefits from someone willing to reconsider assumptions, listen and make a decision using the information available.

Confidence in Learning, Rather Than Prediction

For me, confidence increasingly comes from knowing I can learn and respond. Curiosity supports that confidence: new information becomes useful rather than something to resist.

Setbacks still feel difficult. Adaptability does not remove them, but it gives me something to do with them—revise a plan, practise a skill or try a different approach.

What I Want to Keep Practising

My project and the negotiations made adaptability feel less like a leadership slogan and more like everyday work. I want to keep developing the habit of learning, listening and adjusting. Those habits give me a way to face changes I cannot yet predict.
