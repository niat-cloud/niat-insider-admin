Introduction

Chaitanya Hyderabad management has placed QR codes in every classroom to make student feedback instant and hassle-free.

Students can scan a code with their phone and submit a concern immediately — no form to find, no office to visit, no waiting for somebody to ask.

How the System Works

Students scan the QR code using their mobile phone and submit their concerns instantly.

That lets management see the problems students are actually facing, whether those are academic, to do with infrastructure, or about other facilities. The point is that the concern reaches somebody while it still matters.

Why It Changed Things

This is the part worth understanding.

Earlier, students often found it difficult to communicate an issue, or simply hesitated to report one at all. The reasons are familiar: you are not sure who to tell, or whether it is worth making a fuss, or whether your name will be attached to it.

The QR system provides a quick, simple and confidential way to raise something without that hesitation. It also means every student gets an equal opportunity to be heard — not only the confident ones, which is usually how informal feedback works.

What Management Gets Out of It

The system lets management collect feedback in an organised way and act on it more efficiently.

By seeing which problems come up repeatedly, the college can identify the common ones and work on real solutions rather than reacting to whoever complained most recently.

What the Feedback Showed

The management reviewed the responses submitted through the QR codes and shortlisted the major issues students were facing.

Sports facilities came out as the most significant area needing improvement. Many students raised concerns about the lack of proper equipment and infrastructure — and because the feedback was collected systematically, the scale of the concern was visible rather than anecdotal.

The Basketball Rings Are In

Taking that feedback seriously, the college started renovation work, and the first result is done.

The most important single issue was the absence of basketball rings — and their installation has now been successfully completed, a positive step towards encouraging sports activities on campus.

That is the detail worth noting. A feedback system that produces a visible change is a feedback system students will keep using.

What Students Have Asked for Next

Students have requested essential sports equipment including:

  • Tennis balls
  • Basketballs
  • Volleyballs
  • and more

The management has acknowledged these requests and is expected to provide them soon.

The Badminton Court Is Next

Another issue raised was the lack of proper lining on the badminton court. The management has assured that this work will also be completed shortly, which should improve playing conditions.

Some minor issues still need attention as well. They will be shortlisted and resolved in the coming days.

Conclusion

Putting QR codes in classrooms is a straightforward, modern idea that strengthens the connection between students and management. It has made it easier for students to raise a concern, and easier for the management to act on one in good time.

It makes the campus more responsive, and more student-friendly, by lowering the effort required to say something is wrong.

The basketball rings are the proof. Everything else on the list is now a test of whether the system keeps working.
