Introduction

When I first stepped onto the NIAT campus, I saw programming as a series of syntax and logic. That was it.

Over the last few years I have realised that being a "technical" student is about much more than writing Python scripts. My time at Annamacharya University taught me it is about solving problems — and about bringing people together through code.

The Project: Building the Dream, and Fixing the Bugs

The vision. I wanted to create something interactive — not a static board, but something that felt alive, with AI-driven moves.

The bug moment. I remember struggling for hours with the canvas coordinate system. My "snake" would send the player to a random spot in the middle of the screen instead of the correct tile. For hours.

The lesson. Debugging taught me patience, which is not a lesson I went looking for. And there is no better feeling than the "Aha!" moment when the logic finally clicks and the whole thing runs perfectly.

Leadership Lessons: The Presidential Hustle

Running for a leadership position — President of Entrepreneurship — taught me things no textbook could.

Organisation is everything. Planning an event like Fresher's Day means managing a thousand tiny details, from poster designs through to venue logistics at the main building ground. Miss one and people notice.

Communication is listening. I learned that being a leader is not about giving orders. It is about listening to your "stars" — the juniors — and making sure everybody feels welcome.

Resilience. Campaigning is tough. You have to present your vision clearly and then take feedback with a smile, including feedback you disagree with.

Conclusion

If you are a junior reading this: do not just stay behind your laptop.

Build a game. Attend the workshop. Run for the club position. The bugs you find along the way are exactly what will make you a better engineer — and a better leader.
