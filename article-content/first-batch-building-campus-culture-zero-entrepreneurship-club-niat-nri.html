Introduction

Most students arrive at college and inherit something. Traditions, warnings about which subjects are hard, a senior who tells you how things work.

We arrived and inherited nothing. "The first few weeks were surreal. You'd walk around campus and realize nobody really knew what came next." There were no seniors, no established structures and no traditions to follow — which was frightening and freeing at the same time, usually within the same hour.

No Seniors, No Roadmap, Just Us

Being the inaugural cohort means there is nobody above you to copy.

Nothing had been done before, so nothing had a known answer. No senior could tell us which path to take, because no senior existed. That is the part people underestimate: not having anyone to ask is frightening, but it also means nobody can tell you that something is impossible here, because nobody has tried it yet.

The Entrepreneurship Club Moment

As VP of the Entrepreneurship Club, I had to build an organisation's culture from nothing at all.

There was no previous committee to hand anything over. No format for how meetings run, no calendar of events, no sense of what the club was supposed to be. And then the thought that changed how I treated the whole job: "years from now, students will join this club because of what we started."

That is a strange thing to carry. It is also the best reason to do it properly.

What a First Batch Is Actually Building

The first batch does not just attend a college. It decides what the college is.

The values, the celebrations, the traditions that every future batch will inherit — all of that is being set right now, mostly by people who do not realise they are setting it. Someone will follow a tradition in five years without knowing it started because a few of us decided something on an ordinary afternoon.

The Real Test

The test is not whether this year goes well.

It is whether what we build outlasts us. Foundations matter more than any single year, because they shape the campus's permanent identity long after our batch has left.

Conclusion

"We're not just studying here. We're writing the first chapter."

Everything after us gets written on top of it.
