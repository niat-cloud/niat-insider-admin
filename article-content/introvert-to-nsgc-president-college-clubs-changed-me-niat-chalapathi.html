Introduction

I came to NIAT Chalapathi as an introvert. Seriously.

I sat at the back of the classroom, rarely raised my hand, and assumed the next four years would be lectures and exams. Something happened here that I never expected — the campus pushed me out of that shell, and now I am the NSGC president, the NIAT Student General Council, organising club activities every single month.

From Shy to Running the Show

I did not wake up one day and decide to become a leader. It happened gradually.

The college environment, the way teachers encourage you, the way clubs just exist everywhere — all of it matters. Friends invited me to events and I showed up. Then I volunteered for something small. Before I knew it I was in meetings actually talking about ideas instead of only listening.

NIAT Chalapathi gave me space to grow, and that is genuinely rare. A lot of colleges are only about grades and placement numbers. Here there is actual emphasis on who you become as a person, and the teachers are not just helpful in class — they believe you can do more than you think you can.

What Four Club Activities a Month Actually Means

People ask what I do as NSGC president. The honest answer is that we organise a lot.

Four club activities every month sounds like a lot until you see how much a college needs it. Every week something different is happening — one week a hackathon, another a tech workshop, then a cultural fest, then something completely random that brings people together.

The best part is watching people show up. Freshers who have no idea what to do. Seniors about to leave. All of them turning out for something that has nothing to do with marks or GPA. That is the college life people actually remember.

Why This Matters More Than Academics

Here is what I have learned: the most important skill you gain in college has nothing to do with your curriculum.

It is how you handle people. How you organise things. How you stay calm when an event is falling apart two hours before it starts. How you motivate people who are tired and would rather be elsewhere.

  • You learn to speak in front of people without dying inside
  • You figure out how to make a decision when everyone has a different opinion
  • You understand that showing up and doing the work matters far more than being perfect
  • You realise that half the things you are afraid of are not that scary once you try them

NIAT Chalapathi gave me the platform to learn all of that. Not in a classroom — in real situations, with real stakes, with real people depending on me. That changes you.

The Fests You Don't See Coming

The fests here are genuinely insane, and I mean that well. There is so much energy and so much creativity.

When you are organising them you see the work behind it — the all-nighters, the last-minute decisions, the arguments about logistics that somehow end up improving things rather than wrecking them.

But the best part is watching it come together. Watching people who were strangers two weeks earlier turn into a team. Watching someone discover a talent they did not know they had.

Conclusion

I would tell any fresher reading this: do not come to NIAT Chalapathi just to study and leave.

Show up to events. Join a club. Say yes when somebody invites you to something you have never done before. That is where the real college experience is. The textbooks teach you facts — the campus teaches you how to actually live.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What does NSGC stand for?

The NIAT Student General Council — the student body that organises club activities and events.

Q2. What does the NSGC president do at NIAT Chalapathi?

Organises around four club activities a month, including hackathons, tech workshops and cultural fests, and handles the logistics behind each one.

Q3. Can an introvert become a student leader?

Yes. This student sat at the back of the class and rarely spoke when he arrived, and became NSGC president. He describes it as gradual — showing up to events, then volunteering for something small.

Q4. What events happen at NIAT Chalapathi?

About four a month: hackathons, tech workshops, cultural fests and other activities that bring students together.

Q5. What skills do you actually gain from running clubs?

Speaking in front of people, making decisions when everyone disagrees, staying calm when an event is falling apart, and motivating people who are tired.

Q6. How do you get involved?

His advice is simple: show up to events, join a club, and say yes to things you have not done before.
