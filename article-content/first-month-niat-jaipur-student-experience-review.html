Introduction

The first 30 days at NIAT Jaipur were messy, intense and sometimes overwhelming. They were also some of the most energising days I have had.

Here is what each week actually looked like, including the parts nobody warns you about.

Week 1: The "Am I in the Right Place?" Phase

The first week felt like someone had handed me a puzzle with no box.

Orientation, new faces everywhere, schedules that looked more like spreadsheets. It was a lot.

But here is what nobody tells you: everyone else is just as lost. That shared confusion becomes your first bonding experience. My batch WhatsApp group went from 0 to 247 unread messages in 48 hours, mostly "guys where is the lab?" and "what's the dress code tomorrow?"

Pro tip: save the campus map on your phone. Trust me on this one.

Week 2: Settling In (Sort Of)

By the second week I had a routine. Sort of.

I knew which chai stall opened earliest. I had a desk in the lab that I had silently claimed. And I had made about three friends who had stuck around. The coursework started picking up pace, and the trainers made it clear this was not going to be a slow ride.

The energy here is different. People are actually building things, not just reading about them. That was the moment it clicked for me — this place is serious, but in a good way.

Weeks 3 and 4: From Surviving to Thriving

By week three I stopped counting days, because I was too busy.

Projects, peer learning sessions, random 11 p.m. "bhai ek cheez samjha" calls — it turned into a whole lifestyle. I gave my first internal presentation, completely bombed the introduction, then somehow recovered and got a solid response. Growth, I suppose.

What genuinely surprised me is how much your batchmates push you. I have learnt more from late-night study sessions with friends than I would have thought possible. NIAT gives you the structure. Your people give you the momentum.

Don't try to do it alone. The person sitting next to you probably has the exact answer you've been googling for 20 minutes.

Conclusion

So was it worth it? Completely.

The first 30 days were messy and intense, and they were also the most energising days I have had in a long time. You will find your rhythm, your people and your reason to keep going. Just give it time.

If you are just starting out: welcome to the chaos. You are exactly where you need to be.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is the first week at NIAT Jaipur like?

Orientation, new people and dense schedules that can feel overwhelming. Most students feel equally lost, and that shared confusion usually becomes the first bonding experience — often through a batch WhatsApp group.

Q2. How does the coursework pace change during the first month?

It picks up from week two, with trainers making clear the programme moves quickly. Students spend time in labs building things rather than only reading, and by weeks three and four there are projects, peer learning sessions and informal late-night study calls.

Q3. Do NIAT students get help from batchmates in the first month?

Yes. Late-night study sessions and impromptu calls to clear doubts are described as a major part of the first 30 days, and many students learn as much from peers as from the structured coursework.

Q4. What kind of activities happen in the first 30 days?

Orientation, settling into a routine, coursework and projects, peer learning sessions and internal presentations. This student bombed the introduction of his first presentation and recovered to get a solid response.

Q5. What is the one practical thing a new student should do?

Save the campus map on your phone. It is the article's own first piece of advice, and the campus is large enough that it matters.
