Introduction

When I first joined NIAT Vijayawada, I was nervous, quiet, and hesitant to speak in front of anyone.

Like a lot of students, I had self-doubt, and I avoided anything that meant leaving my comfort zone. Talking to new people felt difficult. Taking part in activities felt intimidating. At that stage I never once imagined holding a leadership role — my entire focus was on adjusting to a new place. This is how that changed.

The Beginning: Fear and Introversion

In the first weeks, my only goal was to not stand out.

Interacting with new people felt hard. Participating in anything felt worse. I was managing, not growing, and if you had told me then that I would stand in an election within the year I would not have believed you.

The Environment That Changed Things

The turning point was not something I did. It was the atmosphere around me.

At NIAT the environment is open and supportive — no judgement, no unnecessary comparison, no pressure to be perfect. Instructors and mentors encourage students to express themselves, and they treat us like companions rather than subordinates.

Because of that, I started feeling comfortable without having to force it. I worked out that it was fine to make mistakes and learn from them, which sounds obvious and is not obvious at all when you are afraid of being wrong in public.

Taking the First Small Risks

Growth does not happen without risk, so I started small.

Attending workshops. Working on projects. Talking to classmates. Each one was minor on its own, and each one made the next one easier. I began communicating more, sharing ideas, joining group discussions.

It was not easy at the start. What made the difference was consistency — doing it again the next day, and the day after. Slowly the fear started turning into confidence.

How Club Activities Changed Me

Club activities played the biggest part in the transformation.

There are regular club events at NIAT where students can show their talents and ideas. I chose the Arts Club, because it gave me a space to express myself rather than perform for anyone.

Those activities put me in front of more people, taught me how teamwork actually functions, and built my communication. Most importantly, they gave me the courage to be seen and heard. The more I took part, the more confident I got.

Standing for Election

Then I made a bigger decision: I contested the club elections.

This was enormous for me, because the person I had been a few months earlier would not have considered it for a second. Standing for a position meant confidence, communication and believing I deserved it.

I was still nervous. I did not let that stop me. I pushed forward and gave it everything.

That was the moment I genuinely left my comfort zone, rather than peering out of it.

Becoming Vice President

A journey that started in fear and hesitation ended somewhere I never expected: I was elected Vice President of the Arts Club.

The achievement is not the position. It is the transformation underneath it — the confidence I built, the fears I got past, the growth I can actually point to.

Someone who was once afraid to speak now leads, communicates and takes responsibility. Those are the same person.

What I Learned

Confidence is not something you are born with. It is something you build.

With the right environment, support and willingness to try, anyone can grow. These are the four things that worked for me:

  • Taking small steps, consistently — the step size matters far less than the repetition
  • Stepping out of the comfort zone — deliberately, not accidentally
  • Taking the opportunities in front of you — most of them look intimidating before they look worthwhile
  • Believing in myself — the hardest one, and the one the others build

Most of all, I learned that growth starts the moment you decide to challenge your fears instead of managing around them.

Conclusion

The person I was on my first day and the person I am now are not the same.

College gave me more than an education. It gave me confidence, courage and something closer to a new identity. That is the proof I would offer anyone who thinks they are "just quiet": college is not only about academics. It is about becoming a better version of yourself, and the version you arrive as is not the one you have to leave with.
