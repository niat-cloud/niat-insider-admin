The Survival & Food Fest at NIAT × NSRIT turns a familiar campus day into a student-run marketplace. Teams plan and manage food stalls, combining creativity with the practical decisions involved in running a small venture.

Building a Mini Business

The work includes planning menus, decorating stalls, promoting products and handling customers. A simple idea needs several people’s contributions before it becomes a stall ready for visitors.

Attracting attention is only part of the task. Teams also have to manage resources and work together when the event becomes busy.

Making Decisions Under Pressure

The “survival” element gives the activity its challenge. Students need to think strategically, respond to problems and share responsibilities. The event creates room to practise collaboration and leadership outside a classroom assignment.

Food and Friendly Competition

Students move between stalls, try different flavours and support their friends. The atmosphere brings together conversation, competition and the smell of freshly prepared food.

Classmates become teammates for the day. The shared effort of turning plans into something visible adds to the memory of the event.

What the Experience Leaves Behind

The stalls eventually close and results are announced, but the account’s emphasis is on the work students did together. Menu planning, customer interaction and quick decisions give the fest a practical-learning dimension alongside the fun.

Frequently Asked Questions

Teams handled menus, decoration, product promotion, customers and resources while working together under time pressure.

What skills does running a stall involve?

The account highlights planning, teamwork, communication and making decisions while managing limited resources and a busy event.

How did teams try to attract visitors to their stalls?

The account describes colourful decoration, themes and product promotion alongside planning the food they would serve.

What did students experience while visiting the stalls?

They tried different flavours, supported friends and spent time in the busy, festive atmosphere around the marketplace.

What remained meaningful after the stalls closed?
