If someone asked me to describe my first 30 days at NIAT × NSRIT in one word, it would be "chaos"—but the good kind of chaos that makes college life exciting, memorable, and worth experiencing.

New Faces, Classrooms and Deadlines

From the moment I stepped onto the campus, every day felt like an adventure. New faces, new classrooms, new schedules, and endless introductions made the first few days a whirlwind of emotions. One moment I was trying to find my classroom, and the next I was making friends who would soon become an important part of my college journey.

The chaos didn't stop there. There were assignments to complete, classes to attend, labs to explore, clubs to join, and events happening all around the campus. Every day seemed packed with something new. Somehow, between managing deadlines and learning new concepts, there was always time for laughter, conversations, and unexpected moments.

Finding Friends in the Cafeteria

The cafeteria became the headquarters of this beautiful chaos. Tables filled with students, noisy discussions, random debates, last-minute assignment planning, and endless jokes created an atmosphere that was impossible to forget. What started as a place for meals quickly became a place where memories were made.

Exploring Ideas in the Gen AI Club

The club activities added another layer of excitement. As a member of the Gen AI Club, I found myself surrounded by creative minds discussing futuristic ideas, planning activities, and exploring new technologies. Every meeting brought fresh energy and inspiration.

When the Chaos Started to Feel Familiar

There were moments of confusion, moments of pressure, and moments when everything seemed to happen at once. Yet, those very moments taught valuable lessons about teamwork, adaptability, and confidence. Slowly, the chaos stopped feeling overwhelming and started feeling like home.

Looking back, the first month was unpredictable, full of learning and sometimes overwhelming. The friendships, conversations and club meetings helped me find my place. By the end, the same campus that had felt confusing was beginning to feel like home.
