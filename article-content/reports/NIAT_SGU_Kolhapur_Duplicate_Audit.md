# PRIORITY 1 — FIX THIS WEEK

## 1.1 A factual error: the institution's own name is wrong

**URL:** `/niat-kolhapur-campus-sahyadri-hills-student-review`

The article expands NIAT as **"National Institute of Advanced Technologies"**. It is
**NxtWave Institute of Advanced Technologies**.

**Action: REMOVE FROM THE PAGE.** Correct the expansion.

This matters more than its 72 views suggest. A wrong institutional name on the institution's own
site is the kind of error that gets copied by aggregators and repeated by AI assistants, and it is
far harder to retract than to fix. It is the same class of error as the NSGC misexpansion found at
Jaipur ("National Student Governance Council" for NIAT Student General Council), which suggests
nobody has a canonical glossary to write from.

**Also on this page:** a UNESCO World Heritage reference. The Western Ghats carry that designation;
the campus does not. Check the phrasing cannot be read as the campus being a World Heritage site.

# WHAT NOT TO DO

**Do not redirect the `rushikesh-the-vlogger` series.** Six articles covering day one, week one, the
first month and the daily routine look like duplication from the slugs and are not. They are a
genuine chronological series, 700–1,050 words each, and densely specific — Gate Number 4, the
five-floor SG building, a Rolls Royce parked on campus, an aircraft near the main building, 7am mess
queues, mess hours of 6:45–9am, a 20–30 minute walk back, poha and upma, DSA and calculus,
Cambridge International School, a classmate named Akhil.

That is the best sustained first-person writing on any campus reviewed so far. It should be
cross-linked as a series with previous/next navigation, not consolidated.

**Do not merge the four "first year" articles.** They are four different students on four different
aspects — curriculum (sena-niatian), the overall honest review (chillmahesh), AI and Python
(Devalapura Praveenkumar), and the daily routine (rushikesh-the-vlogger). Four perspectives is a
strength; four copies would not be.

**Do not pad anything to clear 450 words.** Six of the eleven short articles are fixed by merging.
The remaining five need their authors, and the three questions that have worked elsewhere: what is
one specific thing you did, who was involved and what did they say, and what is one number, date,
place or name you could add.

# STILL THE BIGGER PRIORITY

This campus took ten web fetches and most of a session to audit, and it produced one true duplicate.
NSRIT produced seven URLs sharing a single body in nine fetches.

**A query grouping every article by a hash of its body text would find both results in minutes, across all ~4,000 articles.** It remains the single highest-value thing available, and it is more
reliable than me reading pages one at a time — my slug-based predictions for this campus were right
once in three.
