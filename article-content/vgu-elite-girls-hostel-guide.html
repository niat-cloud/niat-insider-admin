Introduction

Every student arrives at hostel life with a well-planned vision: I will stay focused, follow a routine, and become my most productive self.

Fast forward a few months and that vision quietly adjusts. Late-night conversations become therapy sessions, "borrowing" things becomes a daily habit, and somehow everyone knows where your stuff is — except you.

After more than six months at VGU's out-campus Elite Girls' Hostel, I can say it offers a genuinely safe and peaceful environment, especially compared with the rush of the in-campus hostels. Here is everything I wish I had known before moving in.

The Buildings and the Rooms

There are two buildings:

  • Elite 1 (Nature's Home Maple) — 5 floors
  • Elite 2 (Nature's Home Saffron) — 4 floors

Each floor has around 10–12 AC double-sharing rooms with attached washrooms, which gives you both comfort and a bit of personal space — a combination that is harder to find than it sounds.

Facilities and Daily Living

The hostel has the essentials for a routine that actually works:

  • A refrigerator on each floor
  • A common living area on each floor, which usually turns into a mini hangout zone
  • An elevator
  • Washing machines for laundry, which saves a surprising amount of time

On cleaning: corridors are cleaned daily, while rooms and washrooms are cleaned once a week — usually Sundays, just in time.

The Mess Food, Honestly

Let us be honest: mess food is always a topic of discussion.

Here it is good and decent, and the big advantage is the smaller number of students. Less crowd, and sometimes even the option to request small menu changes.

So yes — fewer people means slightly better chances of getting your favourite meal.

Electricity: A Shared Responsibility

This is the thing nobody tells you about.

Each room gets a monthly electricity recharge of ₹600. If usage goes over that, students may need to contribute extra — roughly ₹300, depending on how heavily the AC has been running.

There is a meter on the ground floor, and it is worth keeping an eye on it to avoid an unexpected power cut-off.

In simple terms: AC is your best friend, and also your biggest responsibility.

Transport, and the Missed-Bus Backup Plan

The hostel is about 4.6 km from the university, and transport is fairly well sorted.

College buses:

  • Morning: 7:45 a.m. – 8:30 a.m.
  • Evening: 4:40 p.m.

But let us be real — missing the bus happens.

That is where the local option comes in: "Magics", the shared vans.

  • Cost: ₹10–20 per ride
  • Pick-up points: SKIT Gate 1 in the morning; VGU Gate 2 in the evening, until 5:30 p.m.

They are affordable, quick, and honestly part of the hostel experience. You can also book a cab, bike or auto if you would rather.

What Is Nearby

The hostel sits in a student-friendly area, so the everyday things are all close:

  • Grocery and daily essentials
  • Stationery shops
  • Tailors, for emergency fixes
  • Cafés and food spots
  • A gym
  • Clinics and medical shops

There is also TFG Cafe in the basement of Elite 1 — good for relaxing, catching up with friends, or just getting out of your room for a while.

And every Sunday there is a local Sunday Market nearby, where fruit and daily essentials are very cheap. It is something students learn to look forward to quickly.

Safety, Rules and the Support System

Safety is a strong point here, with 24/7 security guards.

Entries are logged every time you go out or come in. The rules in short:

  • Gate closing time: 6:30 p.m.
  • Late entries are allowed with permission during college events and other occasions
  • Outings require an approved outpass

Because the hostel has fewer students, some rules feel more manageable and slightly more flexible than in the in-campus hostels. Complaints and issues can also be raised directly with the wardens, which makes getting something fixed faster and more transparent.

One small but genuinely helpful perk: if you are not around when a parcel arrives, you can contact the guards and they will handle it.

In-Campus or Out-Campus? The Honest Comparison

Both come with their own advantages and challenges, and it is worth understanding them before choosing.

In-campus means being closer to classes, which saves time and effort. But it also means a more crowded environment — the mess at peak hours, the rush at hostel gates that are often a long way from the academic blocks just to collect a parcel. Lunch means relying on the in-campus mess, which can feel repetitive or crowded.

Out-campus makes things like parcel deliveries and a less crowded mess much easier. The trade-off is adjusting to fixed transport timings and planning your movements around them.

Conclusion

At first it is just a hostel. Then it becomes a routine.

In the end it is less about which hostel is "better" and more about what suits your own way of living. If you want to be two minutes from class, in-campus wins. If you want space, a quieter mess and rules that breathe a little, out-campus is worth the bus timetable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What facilities does VGU's Elite Girls' Hostel offer?

Each floor has a refrigerator, a common living area and an elevator, with washing machines for laundry. Rooms are AC double-sharing with attached washrooms, around 10–12 per floor, across two buildings — Elite 1 (5 floors) and Elite 2 (4 floors). Corridors are cleaned daily; rooms and washrooms once a week, usually Sundays.

Q2. How does transport work from the Elite Girls' Hostel to the university?

The hostel is about 4.6 km away. College buses run mornings from 7:45 a.m. to 8:30 a.m. and evenings at 4:40 p.m. If you miss the bus, shared vans called "Magics" cost ₹10–20 per ride, picking up at SKIT Gate 1 in the morning and VGU Gate 2 in the evening until 5:30 p.m. Cabs, bikes and autos are also available.

Q3. What is the electricity arrangement?

Each room gets a monthly recharge of ₹600. If usage exceeds it, students may contribute extra — roughly ₹300 depending on use. A meter on the ground floor lets you monitor usage and avoid unexpected cut-offs.

Q4. What are the hostel rules and safety measures?

24/7 security guards, with all entries and exits logged. Gate closing time is 6:30 p.m., though late entries are permitted during college events or special occasions. Outings require an approved outpass from the warden, and complaints can be raised directly with wardens.

Q5. How is the mess food?

Good and decent. Because there are fewer students there is less crowding, and sometimes the option to request small menu changes — so a better chance of getting your favourite meal.

Q6. Is an out-campus hostel better than an in-campus one?

Neither is simply better. In-campus is closer to classes but more crowded, with mess rushes and distant hostel gates. Out-campus is quieter with easier parcel collection, but you have to plan around fixed transport timings.

Q7. How far is the hostel from Jaipur's railway stations and airport?

Khatipura 3 km, Jaipur International Airport 8 km, Durgapura 8.5 km, Sanganer 11 km and Jaipur Junction 16 km.
