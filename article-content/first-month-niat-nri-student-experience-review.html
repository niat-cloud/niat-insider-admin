Introduction

Joining NIAT x NRI Vijayawada was honestly terrifying.

New environment, new people, and a completely different way of learning — I had no idea what I was walking into. I am an introvert, so none of this came naturally to me. Thirty days later I was asking questions in class without thinking twice about it. This is what happened in between.

The First Few Days: Observing, Not Participating

In the beginning I was just trying to adjust, observe, and work out how things functioned.

Within a few days, though, I realised this place was different from what I had pictured a college to be. That was the first thing that made me curious instead of only nervous.

Getting Past the Fear as an Introvert

The early days were a mix of curiosity and hesitation. Being an introvert, I found it genuinely difficult to interact and express myself openly.

The environment made it easier than I expected. The instructors and my classmates were friendly in a way that did not demand anything from me, and that helped me feel comfortable before I had decided to be. Slowly I started opening up — asking questions, joining conversations, saying things out loud.

A Completely Different Way of Learning

One of the biggest surprises of the first 30 days was the learning approach itself.

It was not about sitting and listening. It was about understanding and doing. From the start we were introduced to practical learning, and instead of memorising concepts we were pushed to apply them.

That changed how the work felt. It did not feel like pressure. It felt like exploration.

My First Steps in Programming

In these 30 days I started with Python, HTML and CSS — all completely new to me, and all more exciting than I expected:

  • Python showed me how programming actually works
  • HTML taught me how to structure a web page
  • CSS let me design and style what I had built

Even at that early stage I could see myself making something real, and that is what made me want to keep learning.

The Support System That Made the Difference

What stood out most in my first month was the support.

Instructors were approachable and genuinely ready to help. There was no hesitation about asking a doubt and no fear of being judged for it — which, if you are an introvert, is the single thing that decides whether you ever speak up at all. They guided us patiently and pushed us to improve step by step.

That is what made learning here smooth instead of stressful.

Life Outside the Classroom

The first 30 days were not only academics.

There were activities, interactions and chances to explore different interests. From workshops to club introductions, I got a sense of how active campus life is here — and I understood that learning is not something confined to a classroom.

Conclusion

Those first 30 days were not about achieving anything big. They were about building a foundation.

I picked up basic technical knowledge, my communication improved, and I became more confident. Most importantly, I started believing I could grow. From nervous to comfortable, from confusion to clarity — that is real change, and everything I am learning now sits on top of it.
