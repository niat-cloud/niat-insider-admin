During my first year at NIAT, I began to notice changes that were not measured by grades. I was learning how to take responsibility, respond to setbacks and become more comfortable trying something unfamiliar.

Personal growth happened gradually, through experiences rather than one dramatic turning point.

Taking Responsibility Before Feeling Ready

Presentations, competitions, projects and leadership roles could feel intimidating. Taking part taught me that confidence often develops after you act.

Responsibility also changed how I approached work. When other people depend on a task, deadlines and commitments matter in a different way. I began connecting discipline and accountability with everyday participation.

Learning With Other People

The Gen AI Club gave students opportunities to organize events, work on projects, collaborate and explore ideas. Those activities involved personal development alongside technical learning.

Meeting people with different backgrounds and perspectives mattered too. Some of the lessons that stayed with me came through conversations. Listening helped me see beyond my own assumptions.

What the BRAVE Competition Taught Me

My account of the BRAVE Competition includes nearly 15 unsuccessful business negotiations before a successful 16th attempt. The setbacks were disappointing, but they gave me things to work on.

Communication, confidence and resilience improved through that process. Looking back, I connect the eventual success with what the earlier attempts taught me.

Turning Experience Into Learning

I learned to reflect on what had worked, what needed improvement and what I could carry into the next situation. Experience became more useful when I took time to examine it.

Leadership also started to feel less dependent on a title. Helping someone, taking initiative or contributing to a solution could be an opportunity to practise it.

Curiosity kept that process going. Exploring ideas beyond the syllabus and accepting new responsibilities gave me more chances to learn.

Recognizing the Change

I did not always notice progress as it happened. It became clearer when I compared myself with how I had felt months earlier. For me, one of the valuable parts of college has been recognizing that growth: greater confidence, a better response to setbacks and more willingness to participate.
