During my first year at NIAT, I stopped thinking of student clubs as something that simply filled time after class. They gave me opportunities to take responsibility, practise skills and become part of a community.

Putting Ideas Into Practice

Communication and leadership feel different when other people are involved in a real task. Organizing an event, contributing to a project or supporting an activity requires action, not just understanding the concept.

I noticed students discovering strengths through that participation. Someone might find confidence speaking, another might take to design, and someone else might discover that they enjoy organizing a team.

The Gen AI Club brought students together around artificial intelligence and innovation. Working together involved communication, shared deadlines and practical responsibilities alongside technical learning.

A Community During a New Stage of Life

College can feel unfamiliar and demanding. Shared interests make it easier to start conversations and build friendships. Clubs gave students a reason to connect and a place to contribute.

They could also create unexpected next steps: a discussion might lead to a project, an event or a new interest. The outcome was not guaranteed, but participation made those possibilities easier to encounter.

Learning From Setbacks

The BRAVE Competition gave me a related lesson. Nearly 15 unsuccessful business negotiations came before a successful 16th attempt. At the time, the setbacks were disappointing; later, I could see what they had taught me.

Club work can be similar. The value of a difficult responsibility may become clearer after you have worked through it.

What Participation Changed for Me

Confidence developed through taking part, sharing ideas and accepting tasks. I also began to see initiative as something students could practise by starting a discussion or helping solve a problem.

For me, the lasting value of clubs is in the people, responsibilities and experiences that came with them. They gave me room to learn how I work with others and what I can contribute.
