During my first year at NIAT, I began to understand humility as an accurate view of myself: recognizing what I can do while remaining willing to learn what I do not yet know.

Confidence and humility did not have to compete. I could value progress without assuming I had all the answers.

Learning Through Feedback

The AI-powered infrastructure monitoring and failure prediction project I have been developing required continuous learning and experimentation. New information changed assumptions, and some approaches needed adjustment.

Progress depended on accepting that revision. Feedback was more useful when I treated it as information about the work rather than a judgement I needed to defend myself against.

What the BRAVE Competition Reinforced

Nearly 15 unsuccessful business negotiations came before success on the 16th attempt. Each experience revealed something that needed improvement.

Acknowledging those weaknesses and adjusting the strategy mattered. The lesson I took was that being willing to learn could help me move forward more than trying to protect an image of being right.

Humility in a Team

Listening gives other people’s ideas room to contribute. Sharing credit recognizes the work behind a collective result. Admitting an error makes it possible to address the problem.

Those habits can support trust. They can also keep disagreements focused on a solution instead of becoming a contest over whose opinion wins.

For me, that is part of leadership: making it easier for people to participate and treating contributions with respect.

Staying Grounded as Responsibilities Grow

Humility also connects with adaptability. Someone who accepts that an assumption might be incomplete has more room to change course when circumstances require it.

Remembering other people’s help brings gratitude into that process. Progress is rarely isolated from encouragement, feedback or a teammate’s contribution.

The Habit I Want to Keep

I want confidence in my ability to learn, rather than confidence that I no longer need to. My project work and the negotiations gave me practical reasons to value that distinction.

Humility, as I understand it now, leaves room for further growth. It lets me recognize an achievement while still asking what I can understand or do better next time.
